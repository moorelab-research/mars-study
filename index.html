Menstrual cycle and contraception

Ovarian hormones change how the body handles protein, so this study tests female participants at a specific point in the cycle and can't include anyone using hormonal contraception. If anything is unclear, choose "not sure" rather than guessing.

This includes the pill, patch, ring, implant, injection, and both hormonal and copper IUDs.

The study needs at least 6 months since stopping.

What taking part involves

This is a demanding study and we'd rather you knew that now than at the consent session.

1
Consent and screening — 1 hour, online or in the lab, whichever you prefer.
2
Body composition and strength — 3 hours, fasted overnight. Bod Pod scan, one-rep max testing on six lifts, and a practice run of the workout. Female participants also give a blood sample.
3–5
Three metabolic trial days — 9.5 hours each, fasted, starting around 7am. Each day involves two muscle biopsies from your thigh, a catheter in your arm for blood samples, a full-body workout, and 16 drinks spaced over 8 hours. You can work, read or rest for most of it.
+
Two lead-in days before each trial. We provide all your food, and you do a supervised workout in the lab. That's six lead-in days across the study.
+
Six muscle biopsies in total, two on each trial day. Local anaesthetic, roughly a 5 mm incision each time.
+
Three days of diet and activity tracking, plus one 24-hour urine collection at home.
=
About 32.5 hours in total, and $410 compensation, paid pro-rata if you stop early.

For female participants there's one extra scheduling constraint: trial days have to fall in a specific window of your cycle, so we work around your dates rather than the other way round.

Both are described in full at the consent session and you can ask anything you like before deciding.
